# The bench Z3805A through its states, 2 October 2026

`SmartClockSimulator --watch COM3` asked the bench unit (`SYMMETRICOM,Z3805A,3625A02931,1.01.03-A`,
9600-8-N-1) the same 69 read-only queries every nine seconds or so, while Tony changed the receiver's
state by hand (#639). The folder beside this note holds each run as it was recorded:

- `cycles.md`: every reply of every cycle;
- `screens/`: each status screen whose mode line changed, byte for byte;
- `unsolicited.log.md`: anything the receiver sent with nothing asked, where there was any.

Four of the screens joined the fixture corpus. The simulator was changed to answer as the receiver did
in each state, and its README's table says which rows these runs settled.

| Run | What was done | Folder |
|---|---|---|
| Locked | Nothing: a minute's baseline | `0-locked` |
| Forced holdover | *Force holdover* on the app's Holdover page, then the app disconnected | `1-manual-holdover` |
| Recovery from it | *Recover from holdover*, then the app disconnected | `1b-manual-recovery` |
| Pulled antenna | The antenna cable unplugged at the receiver for about six minutes, then plugged back in | `2-antenna` |
| Power cycle | Power off for about ten seconds, then on | `3-powerup` |
| Power cycle again | The same, with the watch keeping every interrupted reply | `3b-powerup` |

**Two words for holdover.** A forced holdover answers `HOLD`. One caused by losing GPS answers `WAIT`,
with `:SYNC:HOLD:WAIT?` naming what it waits for, while its screen says `Holdover: GPS 1PPS invalid`.
The application shows `WAIT` as "Waiting to recover", which is #642.

**The first minute after the antenna goes is not holdover.** The receiver went on saying `LOCK`, lock
lamp lit, for about 54 seconds with nothing tracked. The time interval answered for the first 18 of
those. The application's "coasting" pill (§10.3) is for exactly this.

## Power-up

Neither power cycle produced the banner §7.2 describes, or a `-362`.

- **First command:** the first byte after power returned was a bare `scpi > `, in answer to whichever
  command happened to be on the wire, with no data and no error queued.
- **Slow screens:** the first status screen took **more than 15 seconds** to start, longer than the
  application's screen timeout, and the second took 7.3. A naive reader is left one reply late; the
  application's cost was one rejected sweep. Since #643 the next command listens for the late
  screen and discards it, so the cost is the screen alone.
- **Refused until lock:** `:SYST:DATE?`, `:SYST:TIME?`, `:PTIM:DATE?`, `:PTIM:TIME?`,
  `:PTIM:TIME:STR?`, `:PTIM:LEAP:ACC?` and `:SYNC:HOLD:TUNC:PRED?`, all with E-230.
- **Refused until a position:** `:GPS:POS?` and `:GPS:POS:ACT?`.
- **Refused for the first half minute:** `:DIAG:IDEN:GPS?`, `:GPS:SAT:VIS:PRED?` and
  `:GPS:SAT:TRAC:COUN?`.
- **Survey:** started at once, being set to (`:GPS:POS:SURV:STAT?` answering `ONCE`, not `1`), with
  progress to a decimal (`+1.8`).

**The two power cycles came up differently.**

- **The first** found satellites in about 40 seconds. During acquisition it showed `ANT DLY 0 ns`,
  `ELEV MASK 0 deg` and starred satellites, as the August capture had, and its survey began at fine
  adjustment.
- **The second** spent six minutes acquiring, and its screen shows four shapes no capture had (now the
  fixture `power-up-survey-suspended-initial-position.txt`):
  - the factory initial position, `INIT LAT N  34:44:00.000`;
  - a survey at `0%`, `Suspended: track <4 sats`;
  - `GPS 1PPS Invalid: inacc position`;
  - a tracked satellite printed `-- ---   --`.

  The simulator calls this a cold start. What made the receiver forget its position is not known.

## Two more findings

- **`:DIAG:QUER:RESP?` repeats the previous command's answer.** It answered the GPS engine's identity
  only because `:DIAG:IDEN:GPS?` came just before it in the list, and `+37015` when
  `:DIAG:LIF:COUN?` did.
- **Single-digit PRNs being attempted print `* 4`.** The star sits in a column of its own. Every August
  capture starred only two-digit PRNs, so this was never visible
  (`power-up-gps-acquisition-single-digit-prns.txt`).

## What was not done

**A health-monitor failure.** None of the six items can be made to fail without harming the hardware;
a pulled antenna leaves the health line at `OK`. The simulator's failing form is still the manual's.

**What the log writes for a forced holdover.** The runs read one log entry each cycle, not the new
ones. The simulator's `Holdover started, manually initiated` is a guess.
